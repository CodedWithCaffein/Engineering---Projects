## Research and original build

I spent many months learning how the parts would work together before making the purchases. I compared performance and cost, but also had to think about the compact case, component fit, cooling, power, and software compatibility.

The original build used an Intel Arc A770 Limited Edition 16 GB. The case is a **Mechanic Master Cool Cube C28**. The purchase records below establish when I ordered parts; they do not establish the exact assembly date.

| Confirmed date | Record |
| --- | --- |
| May 24, 2025 | Ordered the C28 case. |
| May 26, 2025 | Ordered the Arc A770. |
| May 31, 2025 | Ordered the CPU, motherboard, memory, SSD, power supply, cooler, fan pack, and thermal paste. |
| June 30, 2026 | Ordered the MSI RTX 3080 Gaming Z Trio LHR 10 GB. |
| July 1, 2026 | RTX 3080 delivery recorded. This is not a confirmed installation date. |
| October 31, 2026 | System information checked and portfolio documentation updated. |

## GPU upgrade

I later replaced the A770 with the MSI RTX 3080. I wanted stronger performance for my use case and found the card relatively inexpensive at the time. The item price was $300, and the complete order was $349.70.

I still have the A770 on hand, so I have not subtracted a resale value from the project cost. The upgrade is complete, but I do not have a documented before-and-after benchmark to quantify the difference.

## What I learned

The research was a major part of this project. I had to consider the whole system: a part could look good by itself but still affect the budget, physical layout, cooling, or compatibility of the build. Assembling and using the PC gave me a practical way to connect that research to real hardware.

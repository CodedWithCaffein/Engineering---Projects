## Small wind tunnel

**The idea**

I would like to return to my science fair project about how airplane wing shape affects lift and improve it using what I have learned and acquired over the years. Since that project, I have gained experience with CAD, 3D printing, programming, and engineering through school and robotics. I want to apply those skills to a more capable experiment.

**Rough approach**

Build a small wind tunnel with interchangeable wing models and a way to adjust their angle. I could design the models and mounts in Inventor, make parts with my 3D printer, and explore sensors and code for recording measurements.

I would start by checking how consistent the airflow and measurements are, then compare a few wing shapes under similar conditions. The goal would be to improve the setup and quality of the data while seeing how my understanding has changed since the original project.

**Still to decide:** Tunnel size, airflow source, measurement system, and which wing designs to compare.

## Lightweight structure testing

**The idea**

I would like to experiment with small beams, trusses, or brackets and see how much weight I can remove while keeping them useful and strong. This could build on what I have learned from bridge design and CAD.

**Rough approach**

Start with a simple part, make a few versions with different shapes or reinforcement, and compare their weight, bending, and failure under a consistent load. Possible directions include hollow sections, different truss patterns, or changes to 3D-print orientation and infill.

I would keep the first comparison small and change one main feature at a time.

**Still to decide:** The part to test, material, and a simple repeatable loading setup.

## Motorized macro photography rail

**The idea**

A macro photography rail moves a camera forward and backward in small steps. I would like to build a motorized version for my Sony a6300 so I can take a series of close-up photos focused at slightly different distances, then combine them through focus stacking.

I could use it for detailed photographs of small objects, textures, plants, and parts from my engineering projects.

**Rough approach**

Design a camera platform that slides along a guide, moved by a small stepper motor and lead screw. I could make custom mounts in Inventor, 3D print suitable parts, and program a basic controller for adjusting movement distance and step size.

I would begin with manual camera triggering between movements. Automatic triggering could be added later if practical.

**Still to decide:** Travel distance, camera mounting, movement accuracy, and how to reduce vibration.

## Lightweight FPV drone

**The idea**

I want to build a custom lightweight FPV drone powered by a 21700 lithium-ion battery rather than a typical LiPo pack. I have watched a few YouTube videos about similar builds, but most of the ones I have seen use name-brand parts and seem focused on getting the best feel while flying.

My priority would be keeping weight and cost as low as practical, with the goal of maximizing flight efficiency. I want to explore custom frames and very lightweight components, accepting a simpler finish and less refined handling in the early prototypes.

**Rough approach**

Start with an inexpensive prototype, research compatible electronics, and design a lightweight frame and mounts in Inventor. I would choose parts around their weight, cost, and suitability for the power system, then test whether the weight savings actually improve efficiency.

I would gradually revise the frame and component choices based on flight time, power use, and basic handling. Higher-quality parts could come later where testing shows they would make a useful difference.

**Possible timeline**

I would like to begin after this school year and work on it slowly through graduation, making improvements as time and money allow.

**Still to decide:** One cell or a multicell pack, frame size, FPV equipment, budget, and a realistic weight target. Battery connections, charging, protection, and mounting would need to be worked out before building.

## Level 1 rocketry certification

I want to research and work toward Level 1 high-power rocketry certification. The first steps would be finding a club, comparing NAR and Tripoli, and deciding whether to use a junior program or prepare for adult certification after 18. From there, I would choose a rocket, build it with guidance, and arrange a certification flight.

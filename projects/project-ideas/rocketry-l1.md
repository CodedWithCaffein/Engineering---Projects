## What Level 1 means

Level 1 is a hobby certification, rather than a government-issued license. Adult L1 covers H and I class motors; launch-site rules and applicable permissions still apply. Both organizations offer a route before age 18, with adult supervision and additional requirements.

## Two certification options

| Option | Route before 18 | Practical advantages | Things to consider |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **National Association of Rocketry (NAR)** | Junior HPR Participation Program, ages 14–17. Requires current membership, an online exam, parent/guardian consent, an adult flyer of record, and an approved certification team. | A defined junior route; converts to adult L1 at 18 while membership stays current. | The flyer of record must be separate from the certification team. Junior recovery-system restrictions apply. |
| **Tripoli Rocketry Association** | Tripoli Mentoring Program (TMP), ages 12–17. Complete TMP training/exam and consent paperwork, then a mentored certification flight for M1 status. | Also offers a route now; M1 becomes adult L1 at 18 if still a member. | Requires a suitable mentor and a Prefect, TAP member, or Tripoli Director to witness certification. TMP training alone is not M1. |

Neither is an automatic better choice for me. I would compare the clubs I can realistically attend, available mentors, launch dates, and total cost before joining. The most useful option would be the one where I can get consistent help.

## Rough plan

1. **Find a club and attend a launch.** Ask whether it supports NAR junior certification, Tripoli TMP/M1, or both. Confirm who can mentor and certify me before buying a rocket.
2. **Choose the route.** Compare membership, club dues, launch fees, and travel. Decide whether to use the junior route or prepare for adult L1 after turning 18. Adult L1 starts at 18 in both organizations; junior exams are a separate requirement from that adult route.
3. **Learn the basics.** Study stability, recovery, motor classifications, and launch procedures. Review the official study material and complete the required junior exam and consent process if using that route.
4. **Choose a simple certification rocket.** Discuss a suitable kit or conventional design with the mentor. I would favor a straightforward, recoverable flight over trying for maximum altitude. Work out a budget before purchasing.
5. **Build and document it.** Keep photos, notes, and a parts list. Review the rocket and predicted flight with the mentor and certification authority before setting a launch date.
6. **Arrange the attempt.** Confirm the current forms, witnesses, launch requirements, and weather backup. While under 18, the designated adult handles restricted motors and related materials.
7. **Fly, recover, and complete the paperwork.** The flight must meet the chosen organization's rules and pass post-flight inspection. Keep copies of signed forms and submit them through that organization's current process.
8. **Continue learning.** Record what worked, make any needed improvements, and keep membership current. If certified through a junior route, verify the adult L1 update at 18.

## A few things to know

- Each candidate builds their own certification rocket; it is an individual qualification.
- Tripoli's L1 attempt uses one certified H or I motor and parachute recovery. Entirely 3D-printed rockets are not accepted for its certification flights.
- NAR juniors have additional recovery restrictions; confirm the permitted setup with the flyer of record.
- Budget for the rocket, recovery equipment, motor, membership, launch fees, and travel. Prices are still to be researched.
- Passing an exam or launching successfully is not the full process: inspection, witnesses, and completed paperwork matter too.

## Possible timeline

Start with club research and an introductory visit, then work through studying and the build over several months as time and money allow. Leave room for weather delays and another attempt. No certification date is set.

**Still to decide:** Organization, club, mentor, rocket, budget, and whether to certify before or after 18.

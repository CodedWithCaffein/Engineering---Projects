## What I’ve found so far

L1 is a high-power rocketry certification, usually called a certification rather than a license. Adult L1 covers H and I class motors, but I would still need to follow the launch site’s rules and applicable permissions.

Since I’m under 18, I could start through a junior program or spend the time preparing and attempt adult L1 after turning 18. I haven’t decided which makes more sense yet.

## Two certification options

| Option | What I’d need | What appeals to me | What I need to consider |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **NAR — National Association of Rocketry** | Junior program for ages 14–17: membership, online exam, parent/guardian consent, a certified adult flyer of record, and a certification team. | I could start before 18, and junior certification converts to adult L1 at 18 if I keep my membership current. | The flyer of record has to be separate from the certification team. There are junior recovery-system restrictions to check. |
| **Tripoli Rocketry Association** | Mentoring program for ages 12–17: membership, TMP training/exam, consent paperwork, and a mentored certification flight for M1. | Another way to start now. M1 becomes adult L1 at 18 if I’m still a member. | I’d need a mentor and an approved certification witness: a Prefect, TAP member, or Tripoli Director. Finishing TMP training alone doesn’t earn M1. |

I’m leaning toward choosing based on the club and mentors I can actually work with. Travel, launch dates, and cost will probably matter more to me than choosing an organization first.

## Rough plan

1. **Find a club.** Look for launches I could reasonably attend. Ask whether they support NAR junior certification, Tripoli M1, or both, and whether someone could mentor me.
2. **Visit a launch.** See how the process works and talk to people before buying anything. Find out who could witness a certification attempt.
3. **Choose a route and budget.** Compare membership, club fees, travel, and the rocket itself. Decide whether to use a junior program or wait until 18 for adult L1.
4. **Study what I need to know.** Work through stability, recovery, motor classifications, and launch procedures. Complete the junior exam and consent requirements if I take that route.
5. **Pick a rocket.** Go over a suitable kit or design with the mentor. My first goal would be a reliable flight and recovery. I can save more ambitious designs for later.
6. **Build it and keep notes.** Take photos, track parts and spending, and review the rocket and predicted flight with the mentor.
7. **Arrange the certification flight.** Confirm paperwork, witnesses, launch requirements, and a backup date. While I’m under 18, the designated adult would handle restricted motors and related materials.
8. **Fly and finish the process.** Recover the rocket, have it inspected, get the required signatures, and submit the paperwork. Keep copies and check that my membership record is updated.

## Things to remember

- I need to build my own certification rocket.
- Tripoli requires one certified H or I motor and parachute recovery for L1. An entirely 3D-printed rocket would not qualify for its certification flight.
- I need to check NAR’s junior recovery restrictions with the flyer of record.
- A successful launch still needs the required inspection, witnesses, and paperwork.
- Budget for recovery equipment, the motor, memberships, launch fees, and travel as well as the rocket.
- If I certify through a junior route, keep membership current and check the adult L1 update when I turn 18.

## Timing and open questions

I’d like to work through this gradually over several months, starting with finding a club. I haven’t set a launch date. I’ll need room for weather delays, changes to the build, or another attempt.
